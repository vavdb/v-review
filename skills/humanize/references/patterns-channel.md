# Channel-specific patterns, scoring, and review report

Load only when the channel is LinkedIn, email, or Slack, or when the user asked for a **review** with scores. Core patterns still apply on top of everything here.

## LinkedIn

### Phrase-level
- **Pivot transitions:** "But here's the thing", "And here's the kicker", "Here's what most people miss", "Let me explain", "Here's why that matters".
- **Engagement-bait closers:** "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "What would you add?", "Drop a comment if…", "Repost if this resonates". If the post is worth engaging with, people will. Bait is also treated as spam by the feed.
- **Vulnerability performance:** "I'll be honest", "Can I be real for a second?", "I'll be vulnerable here", "I wasn't going to share this but…". Real vulnerability does not announce itself.
- **Fake humility:** "I'm no expert, but…", "I don't have all the answers, but…", "This might be controversial, but…". Always precedes a confident claim. Cut the disclaimer.
- **Tag-and-thank:** five or more @mentions at the end. One or two genuine tags are fine; a list is reach-farming.
- **Dream-realised language:** "a dream come true", "pinch me moment". Describe what happened.
- **Arrow chains:** `→` to show a process. Reads as a slide. Write the sentence.
- **ALL-CAPS single words:** "We onboarded HUNDREDS of customers." Use the number instead of the shout.
- **"What if I told you…"**, **"Here's what nobody tells you about…"**: recognised templates. Keep only when the payoff is a specific, non-obvious insight only this author has. Otherwise cut the hook and start with the claim.
- **"Read that again." / "Let that sink in."**: found at ~22× normal frequency in generated posts. The insight rarely earns the pause. Cut both.
- **"And honestly?"** before a non-controversial claim. State the claim.

### Structural
- **One sentence per line throughout.** The number-one ghostwriter tell. Group related sentences into 2 to 4 sentence paragraphs.
- **Hook → three-point list → mic-drop closer.**
- **Explaining the algorithm:** "Comment so this reaches more feeds." Just ask, or don't.
- **Vulnerability-bait hook:** a failure story engineered to hook, then a tidy lesson. If real, let it be messy.
- **Negation upgrade:** "We didn't just build X. We built Y." Say what you built.
- **Hyperbole opener:** "X will never be the same." "Everything changed." Start with the specific thing.
- **Common-belief-then-counter opener:** "X is for simple workflows. That's what most people think. They're wrong." Start with the insight.
- **Period. Separated. Emphasis.** Normal rhythm, or earn the emphasis with specificity.
- **Self-intro paragraph at the bottom:** "My name is X and I'm a…" Cut, or weave the one relevant credential into the body.
- **Information-withheld hook:** first two lines deliberately omit the subject to force "…more". "At Netflix we had 1 UNSPOKEN rule. It was about this 1 word." State the rule, then why it mattered.
- **"X is [positive]. [X variant] is a whole different game."** Collapse into one direct sentence about the actual challenge.
- **Cliché proverb opener:** "Work smarter, not harder." Replace with the specific observation that prompted the post.
- **External link in the body or "link in comments 👇":** cuts reach roughly 60%. Describe the resource; add the link in a comment later if at all.
- **Achievement post formula:** (1) emotion word + announcement, (2) team thanks, (3) generic lesson, (4) emoji sign-off. "Thrilled to announce… couldn't have done it without… biggest lesson: consistency… excited for what's next 🚀". Replace with what made this hard, surprising, or specific.
- **Fake dialogue:** CEO/CMO scripted back-and-forth. Direct prose, argument first.

### Hook versus value (why generated posts die after the first hour)
The feed distributes on the hook for the first 30 to 60 minutes, then on dwell time, saves, and substantive comments. Generated posts win the first stage and lose the second: the reader finishes in eight seconds, saves nothing, comments "So true".

Hooks that clear both stages: a specific consequence ("I lost my best engineer yesterday"), one number with personal stakes ("Conversion dropped 40% in a week. Here's what I found"), a contrarian claim with named evidence, a story that ends unresolved.

Hooks that game stage one and kill stage two: information-withheld hooks, "What if I told you", triple rhetorical questions, ALL-CAPS, proverb openers.

**Saves test:** is there one referenceable thing (a named tool, a concrete step, a number with context, a decision the author actually made) someone would come back for? **Comment test:** is there a claim specific enough to disagree with, a trade-off with no obvious answer, or a story without a lesson?

### LinkedIn rewrite rules
Under 1,300 characters short-form, under 3,000 long-form. No hashtag stack (weave one to three, or none). No engagement-bait closer. No arrow chains. Real paragraphs. Emoji only where it carries meaning. No external link in the body.

## Email

### Phrase-level
- **Greeting formulas:** "I hope this email finds you well", "I trust this message finds you…", "Hope you had a great weekend" (to a stranger).
- **Closings:** "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "I look forward to hearing from you", "Thank you for your time and consideration", "Warmest regards", "With gratitude".
- **Corporate filler:** "I wanted to reach out because…", "I'm writing to inform you that…", "Per our previous conversation", "As per my last email", "Going forward", "At your earliest convenience", "Please be advised".
- **Fake personalisation:** "I noticed your company is doing great things in…", "I was impressed by your recent…" with nothing specific behind it. Cite the specific thing or delete the flattery.
- **Hedge language:** "I was wondering if perhaps…", "Would it be possible to maybe…", "I just wanted to quickly check if…".
- **Email vocabulary:** circle back, loop in, touch base, sync up, deep dive, bandwidth, on my radar, double-click on, unpack.
- **Politeness stacking:** more than one thanks, more than one sorry, more than one sign-off phrase.
- **Throat-clearing:** "I'd be remiss if I didn't mention…", "It goes without saying that…".
- **Subject-line templates:** "Quick question", "Following up", "Checking in", "A thought", "[Name], quick thought". Name the content.

### Structural
- More than one ask.
- Ask buried at the bottom.
- Two to three times longer than the purpose needs.
- Opens with context the recipient already has.
- Greeting mismatched to the relationship ("Dear Mr Smith" on the twentieth email).
- Vague CTA ("let me know if you'd like to chat sometime") instead of specific ("Free Tuesday at 2?").
- Reads as a template with blanks filled.
- "PS:" line that is the real pitch.

### Email rewrite rules
Lead with the ask. One ask. Cut to minimum length. Match formality to the relationship. Specific CTA with a time or a yes/no. One thanks. Subject line names the content. Skip the wellness greeting. One sign-off.

### Email clarity check (replaces originality check)
Purpose clear in the first two sentences? Exactly one ask? Could the recipient answer in under 60 seconds? Anything ambiguous that will cause a back-and-forth? Easy way to say yes? Tone fits the relationship? Length fits the purpose?

## Slack

### Phrase-level
- **Over-formal:** "I wanted to reach out regarding…", "Please be advised that…", "At your earliest convenience".
- **Corporate Slack filler:** "Just wanted to flag…", "Wanted to surface this…", "Looping in X for visibility".
- **Hedging in a fast medium:** "Sorry to bother you, but…", "I might be wrong, but…", "Not sure if this is the right channel, but…".
- **Emoji overload:** three or more in a short message to manufacture enthusiasm or soften an ask.

### Structural
- Longer than four or five sentences (should be an email, a doc, or a thread with a TL;DR on top).
- Ask buried under context.
- Greeting + body + sign-off structure.
- Over-explains context the channel already has.

### Slack rewrite rules
Ask first. Five sentences max. No greeting or sign-off. Match the channel's tone. A shared link gets one sentence of context, not a summary.

## Originality check (blog and LinkedIn)

Could anyone with a search engine have written this? Flag: advice any consultant could give without domain expertise; no firsthand experience, customer story, or specific evidence; recycled framing ("the future of X is Y"); the same point made twice without adding depth; no earned authority on display; generic examples where the author has specific ones. LinkedIn extra: a product plug dressed as a lesson; a personal story whose takeaway is a fortune cookie.

## Scoring (review mode)

Score 1 to 10 with one sentence of justification each. Dimensions vary by channel.

| Channel | Dimension 1 | Dimension 2 | Dimension 3 | Dimension 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blog / LinkedIn | AI-likeness (lower is better, target 1 to 3) | Authenticity (8+) | Reader value: is it non-obvious to the audience? (7+) | Domain credibility: does it need specific experience to write? (7+) |
| Email | AI-likeness (1 to 3) | Authenticity to this recipient (8+) | Clarity: purpose and ask unambiguous (8+) | Appropriate tone for the relationship (8+) |
| Slack | AI-likeness (1 to 2) | Naturalness: how this person types (8+) | Clarity (8+) | Brevity (8+) |
| Docs / UI copy | AI-likeness (1 to 3) | Clarity (8+) | Specificity: names the thing (8+) | Register match to the product or codebase (8+) |

If AI-likeness is low but credibility or clarity is also low, say so explicitly: the text is clean but hollow.
